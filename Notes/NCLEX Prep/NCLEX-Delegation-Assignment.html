NCLEX Prep

Delegation, Assignment & Management of Care

The RN may delegate a task, but never delegates nursing judgment or accountability for appropriate delegation.

Core Concept: Delegation questions test who can safely care for each client, which tasks require RN judgment, which tasks can be delegated, and when delegation becomes unsafe.
Always consider: state law, facility policy, and the individual team member's demonstrated competence.
1. Delegation vs. Assignment

Assignment

Giving responsibility for the care of a client to a qualified team member.

Example: An RN assigns a stable client to an LPN/LVN.

Delegation

Giving another qualified person responsibility for a specific task while the RN retains responsibility for appropriate delegation and follow-up.

Example: The RN delegates routine ambulation of a stable client to a UAP.

2. The Three Team Members
  • RN — Registered Nurse
  • LPN/LVN — Licensed Practical Nurse / Licensed Vocational Nurse
  • UAP — Unlicensed Assistive Personnel

Scope of practice varies by state and facility. General NCLEX principle: the more assessment, clinical judgment, teaching, evaluation, or instability involved, the more likely the RN is required.

3. The RN: Think A-T-E
  • A — Assess
  • T — Teach
  • E — Evaluate

These major responsibilities usually require RN-level nursing judgment.

4. RN Responsibilities

The RN should generally care for:

  • Unstable clients
  • Newly admitted clients requiring initial assessment
  • Clients with acute changes or unpredictable outcomes
  • Clients requiring initial teaching or evaluation of teaching
  • Clients requiring nursing judgment or care planning
  • Clients with complex needs or certain high-risk treatments
  • Clients requiring assessment before, or evaluation after, an intervention
NCLEX Rule: Unstable, unpredictable, assess, teach, evaluate = RN.
5. What the RN Should NOT Delegate
  • Initial nursing assessment
  • Nursing diagnosis / care plan development
  • Evaluation of client outcomes
  • Initial client teaching or evaluation of its effectiveness
  • Clinical interpretation of abnormal findings
  • Triage decisions / determining nursing priorities
  • Care of an unstable client requiring ongoing RN judgment
6. The LPN/LVN: Think Stable and Predictable

The LPN/LVN can usually perform nursing care for clients who are stable + predictable, with an established plan of care, within scope and facility policy.

Common LPN/LVN Tasks

  • Routine medication administration
  • Dressing changes / wound care
  • Reinforcement of previously taught information
  • Monitoring stable clients / collecting focused data
  • Routine catheter care, enteral feedings
  • Care of clients with stable chronic conditions
  • Certain sterile procedures
  • Reporting abnormal findings to the RN
7. What Should Generally Stay With the RN Instead of the LPN/LVN?

Think RN when the question includes: initial assessment, initial teaching, new admission, acute deterioration, unstable vital signs, new neurological change, complex clinical judgment, unpredictable outcome, evaluation of care, or development of the plan of care.

8. The UAP: Think Routine and Predictable

UAPs can perform tasks that are routine, have predictable outcomes, and do not require nursing judgment, assessment, teaching, or interpretation.

Common UAP Tasks

  • Bathing, grooming, toileting
  • Feeding stable clients
  • Ambulation, repositioning, making beds
  • Measuring routine intake and output
  • Obtaining routine vital signs and daily weights
  • Collecting certain specimens
  • Basic hygiene / assisting with ADLs
  • Transporting stable clients

The UAP performs the task and reports findings to the nurse — the UAP does not interpret the findings.

9. The UAP Can Collect Data but Does NOT Assess

UAP May

  • Measure blood pressure
  • Record urine output
  • Obtain a blood glucose reading (if permitted/trained)

UAP Does NOT

  • Assess whether the BP indicates shock
  • Determine whether urine output indicates kidney failure
  • Interpret the glucose result or decide the clinical response
Rule: Data collection may be delegated. Nursing assessment cannot.
10. The UAP Can Report, But Not Interpret

Appropriate: the UAP reports "the client's blood pressure is 82/48 mm Hg." Not appropriate: the UAP independently decides "the client is in shock." Clinical interpretation belongs to licensed nursing staff.

11. The Five Rights of Delegation
1
Right Task — Is it routine? Is the outcome predictable? Does it require nursing judgment?
2
Right Circumstances — Is the client stable? Is the situation predictable? Could the condition change rapidly?
3
Right Person — Is the task within their role, and are they trained and competent?
4
Right Direction & Communication — Clear instructions: what to do, when, what to report, when to call the nurse.
5
Right Supervision & Evaluation — The RN must follow up, monitor, evaluate the outcome, and intervene if necessary.
Delegation does not mean giving away responsibility and forgetting about the task.
12. The Biggest Delegation Question

Before delegating, ask: Does this task require assessment, judgment, teaching, or evaluation? If yes → it usually belongs to the RN. If no, and it's routine and predictable → it may be appropriate for delegation.

14. Initial Assessment, Initial Teaching, Evaluation = RN

"Initial" is an important NCLEX word: initial admission assessment, initial postoperative assessment, initial assessment after transfer, initial evaluation of a new symptom — these generally require the RN, because the RN must analyze findings and establish priorities.

Once the RN has assessed a stable client, routine follow-up data collection may often be delegated — but if the client suddenly develops hypotension, chest pain, dyspnea, or confusion, the RN must assess.

Initial teaching requires assessment of learning needs, planning, individualization, and evaluation — it generally belongs to the RN (e.g. new insulin teaching, new ostomy teaching, new anticoagulant teaching). An LPN/LVN may generally reinforce previously taught information.

New teaching = RN. Reinforcement = may be LPN/LVN. Evaluation ("did the intervention work?") always requires nursing judgment and belongs to the RN.
15. Stable vs. Unstable Client Assignment

Stable Client (may go to LPN/LVN)

  • Chronic condition with expected findings
  • Routine postoperative recovery
  • Established treatment plan, predictable outcomes
  • No major change in condition

Unstable Client (stays with RN)

  • Sudden dyspnea, new chest pain
  • Severe hypotension, active bleeding
  • New neurological deficit
  • Rapidly changing vital signs, new confusion, acute deterioration

New Admission & Newly Postoperative = Think RN

A new admission needs initial assessment, priority-setting, medication review, and risk identification. A client returning immediately after surgery may have airway risk, respiratory complications, bleeding, hemodynamic changes, and anesthesia effects — this requires close RN assessment.

Chronic and Stable = Think LPN/LVN

Stable COPD, stable heart failure, routine wound care, stable diabetes, chronic disease with an established plan, routine medications.

16. ADLs, Feeding, and Ambulation

ADLs (bathing, dressing, toileting, eating, grooming, ambulating) → if the client is stable and the task doesn't require nursing judgment, the UAP may usually assist.

Feeding

A UAP may feed a stable client who can safely swallow. But if there's aspiration risk, recent stroke, dysphagia, a new neurological deficit, or difficulty swallowing — the nurse must assess first.

Ambulation

A UAP may ambulate a stable client once the nurse has determined it's safe. Do not delegate the FIRST ambulation after surgery, or when vital signs are unstable, severe dizziness is reported, or mobility status is unknown.

First time or uncertain safety = RN assessment first.
17. Vital Signs, I&O, Weights, Specimens, Wound Care
TaskWho CollectsWho Interprets
Routine vital signs (stable client)UAPRN — must respond to abnormal findings

Do not ask a UAP to "recheck it later and see what happens" when a finding could indicate instability (e.g. BP 80/44 mm Hg) — the RN must assess and respond.
18. Medications and Blood Transfusion

Medication administration generally requires licensed nursing personnel — UAPs do not typically administer medications. LPN/LVN medication scope varies by jurisdiction and policy. If the question asks who should assess before medication or evaluate the response to medication — think RN.

Blood transfusion requires careful RN-level assessment and monitoring; new symptoms require immediate RN evaluation. Do not delegate critical assessment of a transfusion reaction to a UAP.

19. Assign the Whole Client, Not Just the Task

An LPN/LVN is experienced with wound care, but the client with the wound also has new hypotension, confusion, and active bleeding — this is no longer simply a wound-care assignment. The client is unstable.

Rule: Assign the whole client according to acuity, not just according to one task.
20. Delegate the Task, Not the Judgment

The RN may delegate "obtain the client's blood pressure" — but not "decide whether the blood pressure is safe enough for discharge." The second requires nursing judgment.

Clear Instructions Are Required

Poor: "Keep an eye on Mr. Smith." Better: "Obtain Mr. Smith's blood pressure now and report immediately if systolic is below 100 mm Hg or if he reports dizziness." Delegation should include: specific task, time frame, what to observe, what to report.

21. The RN Must Follow Up

After delegation, the RN cannot assume "someone else is handling it." The RN should review findings, assess if needed, determine whether the outcome is acceptable, and modify the plan if necessary.

UAP Reports Something Abnormal

"The client who was talking normally is now very confused." → the RN must assess the client. Do NOT say "check again in an hour."

LPN/LVN Reports Deterioration

Once a client becomes unstable or requires complex judgment, the RN should become directly involved.

22. Never Delegate Just Because You're Busy

Delegation is based on client condition, task complexity, team member scope, competence, and safety — not "I don't have time." Do not delegate a task you would not know how to evaluate; delegation requires accountability.

23. Priority and Delegation Work Together

Before delegating, identify who is unstable — the RN should generally take the unstable client, while stable and predictable clients can be considered for LPN/LVN assignment or routine tasks delegated to UAP.

25. High-Risk Words That Suggest Each Role

Suggests RN

Initial, first, new, suddenly, unstable, acute, evaluate, assess, teach, plan, complex, deteriorating, unexpected

Suggests LPN/LVN

Stable, chronic, predictable, routine, established, reinforce, ongoing care

Suggests UAP

Bathe, feed, ambulate, toilet, reposition, transport, weigh, record, measure, obtain routine vitals (always verify the client is stable first)

26. The "Can Something Go Wrong?" Test

The higher the risk of an unpredictable complication, the more likely the RN should perform the task. Ask: if this client's condition suddenly changes during the task, will nursing judgment be needed immediately? If yes, the task may not be appropriate for delegation.

Practice Walkthroughs

Practice 1 — Which Client to the LPN/LVN?

Which client is most appropriate for the RN to assign to the LPN/LVN?

  • A. A client with new chest pain and hypotension
  • B. A client admitted 10 minutes ago with shortness of breath
  • C. A stable client with chronic heart failure requiring routine medications
  • D. A client who suddenly develops confusion

Rationale: Client C is stable, predictable, and has an established plan. The other clients require RN assessment and judgment.

Practice 2 — Delegate to a UAP

Which task is appropriate for the RN to delegate to a UAP?

  • A. Assess a client's new chest pain
  • B. Teach a client how to administer insulin
  • C. Obtain routine vital signs for a stable client
  • D. Evaluate whether pain medication was effective

Rationale: Routine vital signs are data collection. The other tasks require assessment, teaching, or evaluation — these belong to the RN.

Practice 3 — Task the RN Should Perform

Which task should the RN perform?

  • A. Assist a stable client with bathing
  • B. Obtain a routine daily weight
  • C. Perform the initial assessment of a newly admitted client
  • D. Transport a stable client

Rationale: Initial assessment requires RN nursing judgment.

Practice 4 — Reinforcement vs. Initial Teaching

The RN teaches a client how to change a dressing at home. Which action may be appropriate for the LPN/LVN?

  • A. Develop the initial teaching plan
  • B. Reinforce the dressing-change instructions
  • C. Evaluate the client's overall readiness for discharge
  • D. Perform the initial nursing assessment

Rationale: Initial teaching = RN. Reinforcement = may be LPN/LVN.

Practice 5 — UAP Reports an Abnormal Finding

A UAP reports that a stable postoperative client's blood pressure is now 78/44 mm Hg. What should the RN do first?

  • A. Tell the UAP to repeat it in 2 hours
  • B. Assess the client
  • C. Ask the UAP whether the client looks sick
  • D. Document the value

Rationale: The UAP collected the data. The RN must assess and interpret the abnormal finding.

Practice 6 — Safe to Delegate Feeding?

Which client is appropriate for the UAP to feed?

  • A. A client with a new stroke who has not had swallowing ability assessed
  • B. A client with severe dysphagia
  • C. A stable client without swallowing difficulty
  • D. A client who repeatedly coughs when drinking water

Rationale: Routine feeding may be delegated only when swallowing is known to be safe.

Practice 7 — Task That Should NOT Be Delegated

Which task should NOT be delegated to the UAP?

  • A. Obtain a stable client's daily weight
  • B. Assist with bathing
  • C. Determine whether the client is ready for discharge
  • D. Record oral intake

Rationale: Determining readiness for discharge requires nursing assessment and judgment.

Practice 8 — Who to Assess First

Which client should the RN personally assess first?

  • A. Stable client requesting assistance with hygiene
  • B. Client with chronic arthritis requesting scheduled medication
  • C. Client with new unilateral weakness and slurred speech
  • D. Stable client waiting for discharge paperwork

Rationale: A new acute neurological change requires RN assessment.

Practice 9 — Proper Delegation Instructions

The RN delegates ambulation of a stable postoperative client to a UAP. Which instruction is most appropriate?

  • A. "Walk the client whenever you have time."
  • B. "Determine whether the client is safe to walk."
  • C. "Ambulate the client in the hallway and report dizziness, weakness, or shortness of breath immediately."
  • D. "Change the client's activity order if the client becomes tired."

Rationale: Proper delegation requires a clear task and clear reporting parameters, with no transfer of nursing judgment.

Practice 10 — Appropriate Follow-Up After Delegation

Which action by the RN demonstrates appropriate follow-up after delegation?

  • A. Assuming the task was completed
  • B. Asking the UAP to determine whether the intervention worked
  • C. Reviewing the result and evaluating the client's response
  • D. Delegating the evaluation to another UAP

Rationale: Evaluation remains an RN responsibility.

"The LPN/LVN can care for any client because they are licensed."Look at stability, predictability, required judgment, and scope of practice.
"The RN should do everything."Appropriate delegation is part of safe nursing practice.
"Busiest task goes to UAP."Delegation is based on scope, competency, risk, and judgment required — not time.
Ignoring "first/initial/new/newly"These words often push the task toward the RN.

"Report" means the reporter can decide what it meansUAP/LPN report findings; the RN decides what they mean and what to do.

Final Delegation Check
  1. Who is the client — stable or unstable?
  2. What is the task — routine or complex?
  3. Does it require assessment, teaching, evaluation, or clinical judgment?
  4. Is this initial or new? (If yes, think RN.)
  5. Is the outcome predictable? (If yes, LPN/LVN may be appropriate.)
  6. Is the task routine and repetitive? (If yes, UAP may be appropriate.)
  7. Does the RN need to follow up? (Always, yes.)
Key Rules to Remember
  • RN = assess, teach, evaluate, unstable.
  • LPN/LVN = stable and predictable clients within scope.
  • UAP = routine, repetitive tasks for stable clients.
  • Initial assessment, initial teaching, and evaluation belong to the RN.
  • Clinical judgment cannot be delegated.
  • The UAP may collect data but does not interpret it.
  • The LPN/LVN may reinforce teaching but generally does not perform initial teaching.
  • New, acute, unstable, or unpredictable clients generally require the RN.
  • Routine ADLs may usually be delegated to the UAP.
  • Always consider scope of practice, facility policy, and demonstrated competence.
  • Delegation requires clear instructions, and the RN must follow up and evaluate the outcome.
  • Never delegate a task simply because you are busy.
Final Rule: RN = thinking. LPN/LVN = stable nursing care. UAP = routine tasks. Ask: Is the client stable? Does this require assessment, teaching, evaluation, or judgment? Is the task routine and predictable? Who is the safest person to perform it?

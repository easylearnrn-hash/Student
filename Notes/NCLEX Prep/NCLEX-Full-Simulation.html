NCLEX Prep

Full NCLEX Thinking Simulation

Read → Recognize → Prioritize → Decide → Act → Evaluate. Think like a safe entry-level RN.

Core Concept: This capstone combines every prior strategy. The goal is no longer one skill at a time — it's recognizing which strategy a question requires, often several at once.
Final Rule of the Course: NCLEX does not only ask what you know. It asks what you will do with what you know.
1. The Complete NCLEX Thinking Process
1
Understand the task — look for "first," "priority," "immediate," "expected," "unexpected," "further teaching," "follow-up," "appropriate," "contraindicated," "effective."
2
Identify the client's condition — who is my client, what is happening, what changed?
3
Determine stability — stable or unstable, acute or chronic, expected or unexpected, baseline or new change?
4
Identify the priority — is there an airway, breathing, circulation, neurological, or safety threat? Who cannot safely wait?
5
Assess or intervene? — do I need more information, or do I already know enough to act?
6
Determine who should do it — RN, LPN/LVN, or UAP?
7
Eliminate distractors — unsafe, irrelevant, outside scope, correct but later, based on assumptions, too extreme, or not answering the stem.
8
Compare the final two — which is safer, needed now, and supported by the stem?
9
If NGN, follow clinical judgment — Recognize → Analyze → Prioritize → Generate → Take Action → Evaluate.

3. Master Rules

Priority

Unstable > Stable. Acute > Chronic. Unexpected > Expected. New change > Baseline. Life-threatening > Non-life-threatening. Actual > Potential. These are guides, not automatic formulas — always use clinical context.

ABC

Airway, Breathing, Circulation — but only when there's an actual ABC threat. A chronic respiratory diagnosis does not automatically outrank an acute circulation emergency.

Assess vs. Act

Stable + unclear = assess. Unstable + obvious = act. Immediate danger = protect/intervene. Harmful treatment occurring = stop the harm. After intervention = reassess.

Delegation

RN = assess, teach, evaluate, unstable, new, initial, complex judgment. LPN/LVN = stable, predictable, established plan, reinforcement. UAP = routine, repetitive, stable, ADLs, no nursing judgment.

SATA

Do not count answers. For every option: Is it true? Is it safe? Does it answer the stem? Evaluate each choice independently.

NGN

Notice → Connect → Prioritize → Plan → Act → Evaluate (Recognize → Analyze → Prioritize → Generate → Take Action → Evaluate).

Do not memorize one strategy for every question. Not every item is solved with ABC, "assess first," Maslow, least invasive, or "call the provider." Use the strategy that fits the client, not the one you remember first.
Practice Simulation — 20 Questions
How to use: Each question below mirrors a real full-length NCLEX item. Answer, then read the strategy that was used to solve it.

Q1 — Priority

The nurse receives report on four clients. Which client should be assessed first?

  • A. Chronic COPD, SpO₂ 90%, baseline 88–92%
  • B. Arthritis, requesting prescribed pain medication
  • C. 2 hours after thyroid surgery, develops stridor
  • D. Waiting for discharge instructions

Rationale: New, unexpected, airway-threatening postoperative complication. Strategy used: priority, ABC, expected vs. unexpected, new vs. baseline.

Q2 — Assess or Act

A stable client says, "I feel dizzy," awake and speaking normally. What should the nurse do first?

  • A. Obtain vital signs
  • B. Activate a rapid response
  • C. Prepare for intubation
  • D. Administer an unprescribed medication

Rationale: Stable + unclear = assess.

Q3 — Act First

A client receiving an IV antibiotic develops facial swelling, wheezing, and severe difficulty breathing. What should the nurse do first?

  • A. Ask about seasonal allergies
  • B. Stop the medication and address airway/breathing
  • C. Document the reaction
  • D. Call the client's family

Rationale: Unstable + obvious = act.

Q4 — Delegation

Which task should the RN delegate to a UAP?

  • A. Assess a client's new chest pain
  • B. Teach a client insulin injection
  • C. Obtain routine vital signs on a stable client
  • D. Evaluate response to pain medication

Rationale: Routine data collection on a stable client can be delegated; the others require nursing judgment.

Q5 — LPN/LVN

Which client is most appropriate for assignment to an LPN/LVN?

  • A. Newly admitted client with dyspnea
  • B. Stable client with chronic heart failure, routine medications
  • C. Client with new unilateral weakness
  • D. Client with severe hypotension

Rationale: Stable + predictable = LPN/LVN.

Q6 — Negative Wording

A nurse teaches a client taking warfarin. Which statement indicates a need for further teaching?

  • A. "I will use a soft toothbrush."
  • B. "I will report unusual bleeding."
  • C. "I will take aspirin whenever I have a headache."
  • D. "I will tell my dentist that I take warfarin."

Rationale: "Further teaching" means find the incorrect statement.

Q7 — Baseline

A client with COPD has a baseline SpO₂ of 88–92%. Current SpO₂ is 90%, and the client is comfortable. What should the nurse do?

  • A. Activate emergency response
  • B. Continue monitoring
  • C. Prepare for intubation
  • D. Notify the provider immediately

Rationale: Compare the current condition with the client's baseline.

Q8 — Trend

A postoperative client: 08:00 BP 118/72, HR 88 → 10:00 BP 102/64, HR 102 → 12:00 BP 86/48, HR 124. What is the most important interpretation?

  • A. The client is improving
  • B. The client is becoming hemodynamically unstable
  • C. The client has stable postoperative findings
  • D. The values are unrelated

Rationale: BP falling + HR rising = deterioration. Don't look at one number.

Q9 — SATA

Which findings require immediate follow-up in a postoperative client? Select all that apply.

  • A. Sudden dyspnea
  • B. Mild incisional pain
  • C. New confusion
  • D. Small amount of expected drainage
  • E. Severe hypotension

Rationale: A, C, and E are concerning; B and D may be expected. Evaluate each option individually — don't count.

Q10 — Provider Trap

A client receiving blood develops fever, chills, and dyspnea. What should the nurse do first?

  • A. Notify the healthcare provider
  • B. Stop the transfusion
  • C. Document the findings
  • D. Teach the client about transfusion reactions

Rationale: Stop the source of harm first; notification and further actions follow.

Q11 — Pain Trap

Which client should the nurse see first?

  • A. Postoperative client, pain 9/10, stable vitals
  • B. Client with new confusion and BP 82/46 mm Hg
  • C. Client requesting sleep medication
  • D. Client waiting for a routine dressing change

Rationale: Instability outranks pain when life-threatening deterioration is present.

Q12 — Autonomy

A competent adult client states, "I do not want this surgery." Which response is best?

  • A. "You have to have the surgery."
  • B. "Your family can decide for you."
  • C. "Tell me what concerns you about the surgery."
  • D. "The surgeon knows what is best."

Rationale: Respect autonomy and explore the client's concerns.

Q13 — Therapeutic Communication

A client says, "I am terrified about my diagnosis." Which response is most therapeutic?

  • A. "Don't worry."
  • B. "Everything will be okay."
  • C. "Tell me what worries you most."
  • D. "You should try to stay positive."

Rationale: Use open-ended, nonjudgmental communication.

Q14 — Safety

A confused client begins climbing over the bed rail. Which action should the nurse take first?

  • A. Document the behavior
  • B. Prevent the client from falling
  • C. Call the family
  • D. Begin discharge teaching

Rationale: Immediate safety comes before documentation or teaching.

Q15 — Hypoglycemia

A client with diabetes is awake, diaphoretic, shaky, glucose 46 mg/dL, and able to swallow. Which action is priority?

  • A. Begin diabetic teaching
  • B. Treat the hypoglycemia
  • C. Document the value
  • D. Ask about long-term meal planning

Rationale: The problem is already identified — don't assess what you already know.

Q16 — Seizure

A client begins having a generalized seizure. Which action should the nurse take first?

  • A. Insert an object into the mouth
  • B. Protect the client from injury
  • C. Restrain the extremities
  • D. Ask about seizure history

Rationale: Immediate safety first. Do not restrain, and do not place objects in the mouth.

Q17 — First vs. Later

A client has severe shortness of breath and low oxygen saturation. Which intervention is most important first?

  • A. Provide discharge teaching
  • B. Address oxygenation
  • C. Document the findings
  • D. Discuss long-term prevention

Rationale: Stabilize first.

Q18 — New vs. Chronic

Which client should the nurse assess first?

  • A. Client with chronic back pain for 5 years
  • B. Client with chronic ankle edema, unchanged for months
  • C. Client with sudden slurred speech and unilateral weakness
  • D. Client with stable constipation

Rationale: Acute neurological change outranks chronic, stable problems.

Q19 — RN vs. UAP

A UAP reports, "The client's blood pressure is 78/42 mm Hg." What should the RN do next?

  • A. Ask the UAP to decide whether the client is in shock
  • B. Assess the client
  • C. Document the value without assessment
  • D. Ask the UAP to evaluate perfusion

Rationale: The UAP collects data; the RN assesses and interprets.

Q20 — Initial vs. Reinforcement

Which action is appropriate for an LPN/LVN?

  • A. Perform initial discharge teaching
  • B. Develop the nursing plan of care
  • C. Reinforce previously taught wound-care instructions
  • D. Perform the initial assessment of a new admission

Rationale: Initial teaching = RN. Reinforcement of previously taught material may be LPN/LVN.

4. NGN Case Study Walkthroughs

Respiratory Deterioration

Pneumonia client: 08:00 SpO₂ 94%, RR 20, alert, mild cough → 12:00 SpO₂ 86%, RR 32, restless, increased work of breathing.

Recognize: falling SpO₂, rising RR, new restlessness, increased work of breathing. Analyze: worsening oxygenation/respiratory status. Prioritize: acute respiratory deterioration. Generate/Act: assess respiratory status rapidly, provide indicated oxygen support, position appropriately, escalate if needed. Evaluate: higher SpO₂, lower RR, decreased work of breathing, improved mental status.

Poor Perfusion

Postoperative client: HR 128, BP 82/48, urine output 15 mL/hr, cool clammy skin, new confusion.

Recognize/Analyze: tachycardia + hypotension + low output + cool skin + confusion = poor tissue perfusion. Prioritize: possible shock. Act: address circulation immediately, consider blood/fluid loss, escalate. Evaluate: BP increasing, HR decreasing, output increasing, mental status improving, skin warming.

Hypoglycemia

Glucose 42 mg/dL, tremors, diaphoresis, confusion. Prioritize: symptomatic hypoglycemia. Act: treat immediately based on ability to swallow and level of consciousness. Evaluate: glucose improving, mental status improving, tremors and diaphoresis resolving.

5. Special Item Format Simulations

Bow-Tie

Glucose 44, tremors, diaphoresis, confusion. Center: hypoglycemia. Actions: interventions that directly correct it. Monitor: glucose, mental status, symptoms. Condition → Action → Monitoring must all connect.

Matrix / Grid

Mild incisional pain and stable COPD SpO₂ 90% = expected. Sudden stridor, new confusion, and severe hypotension = concerning. Evaluate each row independently.

Trend

SpO₂ 96%→92%→86%→80% with mental status alert→alert→restless→confused over 6 hours shows progressive deterioration. Direction matters more than one isolated value.

Highlight

Osteoarthritis and seasonal allergies are background. New crushing chest pain, diaphoresis, and BP 82/48 support acute cardiovascular instability — highlight only those.

6. Managing a Large Case Study

Use four passes instead of reading and panicking:

  1. Task — what are they asking?
  2. Red flags — what changed?
  3. Clusters — what findings belong together?
  4. Priority — what threatens the client most?

8. Quick Decision Rules
RuleAsk
No MaybeAm I reasoning from "the question tells me..." instead of "maybe the client..."?
Right NowWhat does this client need right now? (Eliminates long-term teaching, discharge planning, documentation, future follow-up.)
Who Can Wait?Which client can safely wait — eliminate the stable ones.
What Can Kill First?Airway obstruction, respiratory failure, severe bleeding, shock, acute neuro deterioration.
What Changed?Look for "suddenly," "new," "became," "developed," "increased," "decreased," "previously."
Expected or Unexpected?Expected findings move down in priority; unexpected findings move up.
Do I Know Enough?Not enough info → assess. Enough info + unstable → act.
Can the Nurse Do Something?Before choosing "notify the provider," can the nurse safely intervene first?
Is This Within Scope?Does the answer ask the RN to prescribe, diagnose, or make an unauthorized treatment change? Eliminate it.
Is It Too Extreme?Restraints, invasive intervention, security, aggressive treatment — is there a safer option first?
Did It Work?After any intervention: did symptoms, vitals, labs, and mental status improve? Is the client stable?
9. When You Get Stuck
  1. Restate the task in one sentence.
  2. Identify the strongest cue.
  3. Identify the client's condition.
  4. Eliminate two options.
  5. Compare the final two.

One-Sentence Test: "I chose C because this client has a new airway threat and cannot safely wait." If your explanation requires "maybe this happened, and maybe...", you're probably overthinking.

10. Change Answers Only With a Reason

Don't change an answer because "it seemed too easy." Change only if you identify a missed key word, a safety issue, a priority issue, a scope issue, or a clinical fact you initially misunderstood.

13. Final Exam Thinking Routine
  1. Read the task.
  2. Identify the key word.
  3. Find the important client information.
  4. Determine stable vs. unstable.
  5. Look for acute change.
  6. Check ABC / neuro / safety.
  7. Decide assess vs. act.
  8. Check scope and delegation.
  9. Eliminate distractors.
  10. Choose and move forward.
14. Exam Mindset
  • Do not let one hard question affect the next — each question is a new problem.
  • Do not panic over an unfamiliar disease; ask what the client is showing, whether they're stable, and whether the answer is safe.
  • If all four answers look correct, the item is testing priority, timing, scope, or safety — ask "which one is best right now?"
  • If no answer looks perfect, choose the best available answer — do not invent a fifth option.
  • When you don't know the content, use safety: could this harm the client, delay treatment, violate scope, or ignore an acute change?
Knowledge + Strategy = NCLEX Performance. Knowledge alone is not enough. Strategy alone is not enough. You need knowledge + clinical judgment + safe decision-making.

Key Rules to Remember
  1. Read the task before answering.
  2. Look for changes from baseline.
  3. Unstable usually comes before stable.
  4. Acute usually comes before chronic.
  5. Unexpected usually comes before expected.
  6. Use ABC when there is a real ABC threat.
  7. Stable + unclear = assess.
  8. Unstable + obvious = act.
  9. RN = assess, teach, evaluate, unstable.
  10. Do not count SATA answers.
  11. Look for NGN cue clusters and trends.
  12. Do not call the provider before an appropriate immediate nursing action.
  13. Do not add information.
  14. A true answer can still be wrong for the question.
  15. Always ask: what does this client need RIGHT NOW?
Final Rule: Do not answer the diagnosis. Do not answer your imagination. Do not answer what may happen tomorrow. Answer THIS question, for THIS client, RIGHT NOW.

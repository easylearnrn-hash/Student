NCLEX Prep

SATA, Answer Elimination & Distractors

Do not ask "how many answers?" Ask: "Is this individual option correct for this client and this situation?"

Goal: Handle SATA correctly, evaluate every option independently, eliminate unsafe/irrelevant answers, recognize distractor patterns, and compare the final two options without relying on familiarity.
Golden Rule: Content and clinical judgment determine the answer — not pattern recognition, not the number of blanks, not familiarity.
1. What Does SATA Mean?

SATA = Select All That Apply. These questions may have more than one correct answer. Evaluate every option individually.

Do not assume there should be 2, 3, or 4 answers, or think "I already selected enough."

2. The True/False Method

Treat each SATA option like its own true/false question: "Is this statement correct for this client?" If yes, select it. If no, leave it. Do not compare the number of selected answers to some expected count.

3. Don't Chase a Pattern — Don't Force or Remove Answers
  • There is no rule that SATA "usually has three answers" — the number varies.
  • Do not add an answer just because you feel you selected "too few."
  • Do not remove a correct answer just because you selected "too many" (four correct answers is fine if all four are correct).

Rule: Never use quantity as a decision tool.

4. Go Slow — and Let the Stem Control SATA

Evaluate options one at a time (A, then B, then C...) instead of scanning all options for a "group." This prevents one option from influencing your judgment of another.

SATA is not "select every true nursing statement" — it is select every statement that correctly answers THIS question. A true statement can still be wrong if it doesn't answer the stem.

5. Decode the Question First

Before evaluating any options, identify:

  • Who is the client?
  • What is happening?
  • What is the task?
  • Is it a positive or negative question?
  • Is it asking about expected or unexpected findings?
  • Is this teaching, assessment, safety, or intervention?
6. Positive vs. Negative Wording

Negative Wording → Select the Concerning Option

"Requires follow-up," "needs further teaching," "unexpected," "contraindicated," "should be questioned."

Positive Wording → Select the Correct/Expected Option

"Indicates understanding," "expected findings," "appropriate interventions," "signs of improvement," "correct teaching."

7. The Three-Test Method

For every option, ask:

  1. Is it true? Is the statement clinically correct?
  2. Is it safe? Would this action protect the client?
  3. Does it answer the question? Does it match the task in the stem?

If any answer is "no," eliminate it.

8. Elimination Is Clinical Reasoning, Not Guessing

Eliminate an option if it:

  • Creates immediate harm or ignores an ABC problem
  • Delays emergency treatment
  • Requires information not provided in the stem
  • Is outside nursing scope
  • Is correct but unrelated to the question, or correct but appropriate later — not first
  • Uses an unnecessary restrictive intervention
  • Contradicts client safety or ignores client autonomy
  • Ignores a clear abnormal finding
9. The 10 Distractor Types
#Distractor TypeRule
1True but irrelevantRelated does not mean relevant to the stem's task.

10. Least Restrictive & Least Invasive Principles

Least Restrictive

Progression: verbal redirection → environmental modification → close observation → additional interventions → restraints only when necessary and per policy. Don't jump to restraints if safer measures are available.

Least Invasive

If two interventions are both appropriate, prefer the less invasive one — unless it is inadequate for the emergency. Least invasive does not mean insufficient care.

11. Client Safety, Autonomy & Convenience
  • Do not choose an answer because it's easier for the nurse — convenience never determines the correct action.
  • Avoid answers that force treatment, ignore refusal, threaten the client, withhold information, or decide for a competent client without justification.
  • A competent client generally has the right to receive information, ask questions, refuse treatment, and participate in decisions.
12. Therapeutic Communication Distractors

Eliminate

False reassurance ("Don't worry, everything will be fine"), blaming "why" questions, changing the subject, minimizing feelings, judging, giving personal advice, promising secrecy, arguing with delusions.

Prefer

Responses that explore, clarify, acknowledge feelings, use open-ended communication, and maintain safety — e.g., "Tell me what concerns you most right now."

13. Traps That "Feel" Right But Aren't
TrapCorrection
Choosing an answer because it sounds familiarThe stem must prove the answer, not memory.
Keyword matching (same word in stem & option)Understand the relationship, not just matching words.
Assuming the longest answer is correctNo such rule exists — use clinical reasoning.
"All of these sound good" thinkingEvery option must independently satisfy the stem.
14. Final Two Answers — 7 Questions to Ask
  1. Which one directly answers the task in the stem?
  2. Which one is safer for the client right now?
  3. Which one happens first (sequence matters)?
  4. Which one is within nursing scope?
  5. Which one uses only the information provided (no assumptions)?
  6. Which one addresses the client's current condition, not a future or general concern?
  7. Which answer actually solves the problem, rather than just documenting, teaching, notifying, or observing?
15. The "Best of Two" Formula

When two options remain, compare:

  1. Relevance — Does it answer the stem?
  2. Safety — Does it protect the client?
  3. Timing — Is it needed now?
  4. Scope — Can the nurse appropriately do it?
  5. Evidence in the stem — Is the answer supported by what was given?
SATA Practice Examples
How to use: Check every box you believe is correct, then press "Check Answers." There is no fixed number of correct choices.

SATA 1 — Warfarin Teaching

A nurse is teaching a client taking warfarin. Which statements indicate understanding? Select all that apply.

  • A. "I will use a soft toothbrush."
  • B. "I will report unusual bleeding."
  • C. "I can take aspirin whenever I have pain."
  • D. "I will tell my dentist I take warfarin."
  • E. "I should double my dose if I miss one."

Rationale: A, B, and D are correct. C and E are unsafe. Evaluate each option separately — do not stop at "three sounds right."

SATA 2 — Hypoglycemia Findings

Which findings may indicate hypoglycemia? Select all that apply.

  • A. Diaphoresis
  • B. Tremors
  • C. Confusion
  • D. Severe hyperthermia only
  • E. Tachycardia

Rationale: A, B, C, and E are correct — four answers. Do not remove one simply because four "feels like too many."

SATA 3 — Active Seizure

Which actions are appropriate for a client during an active seizure? Select all that apply.

  • A. Protect the client's head
  • B. Place an object in the client's mouth
  • C. Remove nearby dangerous objects
  • D. Restrain the client's extremities
  • E. Maintain safety and observe seizure activity

Rationale: A, C, and E are correct. B and D are unsafe — safety helps eliminate distractors.

SATA 4 — Delegating to a UAP

Which tasks may generally be delegated to a UAP for stable clients? Select all that apply.

  • A. Assist with bathing
  • B. Perform initial teaching
  • C. Obtain routine vital signs
  • D. Evaluate pain medication effectiveness
  • E. Obtain a routine daily weight

Rationale: A, C, and E are correct — routine, predictable, no nursing judgment required. B and D require RN-level judgment.

SATA 5 — Postoperative Follow-Up

Which findings require immediate follow-up in a postoperative client? Select all that apply.

  • A. Sudden dyspnea
  • B. Mild incisional pain
  • C. New confusion
  • D. Small amount of expected drainage
  • E. Severe hypotension

Rationale: A, C, and E are acute, unexpected, and dangerous — that's what "immediate follow-up" is asking for.

SATA 6 — COPD Baseline SpO₂ 88%–92%

Which findings are concerning for this client? Select all that apply.

  • A. SpO₂ 90% at baseline
  • B. New confusion
  • C. SpO₂ 78%
  • D. Severe cyanosis
  • E. Chronic mild exertional dyspnea, unchanged from baseline

Rationale: B, C, and D are correct. Use the client's baseline — don't flag expected chronic findings just because they look abnormal for a healthy person.

Multiple-Choice Practice Examples

Practice 1 — Facial Swelling and Wheezing

A client receiving IV medication develops facial swelling and wheezing. Which action should the nurse take first?

  • A. Document the reaction
  • B. Stop the medication and address airway/breathing
  • C. Ask about food allergies
  • D. Notify the family

Rationale: A comes later, C is an unnecessary assessment during an obvious emergency, and D is irrelevant to immediate safety.

Practice 2 — Stable Client With Dizziness

A stable client reports dizziness. Which action should the nurse take first?

  • A. Assess vital signs
  • B. Prepare for intubation
  • C. Call the client's family
  • D. Administer an unprescribed medication

Rationale: B is extreme and unsupported, C doesn't address the problem, and D is unsafe. Stable + unclear cause = assess first.

Practice 3 — Confused Client Attempting to Get Out of Bed

A confused client attempts to get out of bed without assistance. Which action should the nurse take first?

  • A. Document the behavior
  • B. Prevent the client from falling
  • C. Teach the client about hospital policy
  • D. Call dietary services

Rationale: Immediate safety outranks documentation and teaching.

Practice 4 — Client Refuses Medication

A client refuses a medication. Which action is most appropriate?

  • A. Hide the medication in food
  • B. Ask the client about the reason for refusal
  • C. Tell the client they must take it
  • D. Threaten to notify the provider

Rationale: B respects autonomy and gathers information. The other options are coercive or unsafe.

Practice 5 — Postoperative Pain 6/10, Stable Vitals

Which action should the nurse take first?

  • A. Assess the pain further
  • B. Activate emergency response
  • C. Prepare for surgery immediately
  • D. Call the family

Rationale: Stable + unclear = assess.

Practice 6 — Fever, Chills, and Dyspnea During Transfusion

A client receiving blood develops fever, chills, and dyspnea. Which action should the nurse take first?

  • A. Stop the transfusion
  • B. Document the symptoms
  • C. Reassure the client
  • D. Finish the transfusion quickly

Rationale: Stop the source of harm first.

Practice 7 — Which Client to See First?

Which client should the nurse see first?

  • A. Client with chronic pain 7/10
  • B. Client with stable COPD at baseline SpO₂ 90%
  • C. Client with new stridor after thyroid surgery
  • D. Client asking for discharge paperwork

Rationale: New stridor after thyroid surgery signals a new airway threat, which outranks the other, lower-priority options.

Practice 8 — Most Therapeutic Response

Which response is most therapeutic?

  • A. "Don't worry, everything will be fine."
  • B. "Why are you so upset?"
  • C. "Tell me what concerns you most right now."
  • D. "You should try to stay positive."

Rationale: C explores, is open-ended, and acknowledges the client's concern. A, B, and D minimize or judge.

Practice 9 — "I Don't Want Surgery"

A client says, "I don't want surgery." Which response is best?

  • A. "You have no choice."
  • B. "The surgeon knows what is best."
  • C. "Tell me what concerns you about the surgery."
  • D. "I'll ask your family to convince you."

Rationale: Respect autonomy and explore the concern.

Practice 10 — UAP Reports Low BP

A UAP reports a client's BP is 78/42 mm Hg. Which action should the RN take first?

  • A. Ask the UAP to interpret the result
  • B. Assess the client
  • C. Document the value and continue rounds
  • D. Tell the UAP to decide whether to call the provider

Rationale: The UAP collects data; the RN assesses and interprets it.

16. The "Safe Answer" Rule — and Its Traps
RuleWatch Out For
When uncertain, ask which answer is least likely to harm the client.Safety must still match the stem's task.
"Safe" is not the same as "do nothing."Severe hypoglycemia needs treatment — observation alone is unsafe.
Reassurance is fine after assessment and facts.False reassurance ("nothing to worry about") is not therapeutic.
Teaching is rarely first during instability.Stabilize first (hypoxia, bleeding, seizure, choking, severe hypoglycemia), teach later.
Documentation matters, but not before treatment.If the problem is still occurring, treat first, then document.
New unstable findings need action now.Don't choose "recheck in 30 minutes" for severe hypotension, new confusion, acute dyspnea, or active bleeding.
17. SATA Error Analysis

When you miss a SATA question, ask:

  • Did I look for a certain number of answers?
  • Did I force an extra answer, or remove a correct one because I selected "too many"?
  • Did I treat every option independently?
  • Did I miss negative wording (further teaching, follow-up, unexpected)?
  • Did I choose a true but irrelevant answer?
  • Did I use safety correctly, or add information that wasn't given?
18. Multiple-Choice Error Analysis

When you miss a regular question, ask:

  • Did I choose a true but lower-priority answer?
  • Did I choose something appropriate later instead of first?
  • Did I choose provider notification too early, or an extreme intervention?
  • Did I ignore scope or autonomy?
  • Did I miss an obvious distractor?
19. The SATA Method (Use Every Time)
1
Decode the stem — what is the question actually asking?
2
Identify positive vs. negative wording.
3
Take each option alone — true or false?
4
Check safety — could this harm the client?
5
Check relevance — does it answer the stem?
6
Do not count your answers — select every option that passes.
20. The Elimination Method
1
Eliminate obviously unsafe answers.
2
Eliminate answers outside nursing scope.
3
Eliminate answers that don't answer the stem.
4
Eliminate answers that happen later.
5
Compare the final two using safety, timing, relevance, scope, and client condition.
21. Quick SATA Check (Before Submitting)
  • Did I evaluate each option independently?
  • Did I accidentally use a pattern?
  • Did I force an answer, or remove one, based on quantity?
  • Did I notice "further teaching," "follow-up," or "unexpected"?
  • Does every selected option answer the exact stem?

24. Final NCLEX Check

Before choosing an answer, ask:

  • What is the question asking, and is this positive or negative wording?
  • Is this answer true, safe, and does it answer the question?
  • Is it appropriate now, and within nursing scope?
  • Am I adding information, or choosing it only because it sounds familiar?
  • If SATA, did I evaluate each option independently?
Key Rules to Remember
  • SATA has no fixed number of correct answers — evaluate each option independently with the true/false method.
  • Do not force an extra answer, and do not remove a correct one because you selected many.
  • A true statement can still be wrong for the stem.
  • Eliminate unsafe answers, answers outside nursing scope, and answers that happen later when the question asks "first."
  • Don't choose provider notification before an available immediate nursing action.
  • Don't assess an obvious emergency, and don't intervene without enough information when the client is stable.
  • Respect autonomy; prefer the least restrictive safe intervention when appropriate.
  • Don't choose an answer just because it sounds familiar.
  • When stuck between two, compare safety, timing, relevance, scope, and the client's current condition.
Final Rule: Do not count answers. Judge answers. For every option ask: Is it true? Is it safe? Does it answer THIS question? And when two remain: which one is more correct for THIS client RIGHT NOW?

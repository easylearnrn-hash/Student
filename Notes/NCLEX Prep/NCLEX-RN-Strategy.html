NCLEX Prep

NCLEX-RN Strategy

How to read and understand NCLEX questions — recognizing the task, the key word, and the safest answer

Core Concept: The NCLEX-RN does not only test whether you know nursing information. It tests whether you can understand the situation, recognize important clinical information, and choose the safest and most appropriate nursing action. A student may know the disease, medication, procedure, or lab value and still answer incorrectly if the question itself is misunderstood.
The First Rule of NCLEX Strategy: Do not answer the question until you understand exactly what the question is asking.
1. Identify What the Question Is Asking

Before choosing an answer, identify the task. Ask: "What am I looking for?" Before looking closely at the answer choices, complete this sentence: "I am looking for __________."

Common NCLEX Wording

  • First / Priority / Immediate / Most important
  • Expected / Unexpected
  • Requires follow-up / Requires further teaching
  • Indicates understanding / Appropriate action
  • Contraindicated / Question the prescription
  • Effective treatment

3. Positive vs. Negative Questions
One of the easiest ways to miss an NCLEX question is to accidentally look for the opposite answer.

Positive Questions

Examples: "Which statement indicates understanding?" · "Which intervention is appropriate?" · "Which finding is expected?" · "Which action should the nurse take?" · "Which finding demonstrates improvement?"

You are looking for: the correct, safe, or expected answer.

Negative Questions

Examples: "Which statement requires further teaching?" · "Which finding requires follow-up?" · "Which action is contraindicated?" · "Which prescription should the nurse question?" · "Which finding is unexpected?"

You are looking for: the incorrect, unsafe, abnormal, or concerning answer.

Pay very close attention to: FURTHER TEACHING · FOLLOW-UP · UNEXPECTED · CONTRAINDICATED · QUESTION THE ORDER
4. Identify Who the Client Is

Important Information May Include

  • Age, pregnancy, postpartum status
  • Newborn, child, or older adult
  • Diagnosis, medication, procedure
  • Postoperative status / time since surgery
  • Medical history

However, not every detail in the question is equally important.

Example

A 72-year-old client with hypertension and osteoarthritis is 1 hour postoperative following a thyroidectomy and develops stridor.

Important information: recent thyroid surgery + stridor. The client's osteoarthritis is not important to the immediate situation.

Learn to separate relevant clinical information from background information.
5. Look for What Changed

Watch For These Words

  • Suddenly, newly, developed, became
  • Increased, decreased, now
  • Previously, baseline, usual

Examples of Important Changes

  • Alert → confused
  • BP 130/80 → 84/50 mm Hg
  • SpO₂ 97% → 85%
  • Urine output 50 mL/hr → 10 mL/hr
  • Pain 2/10 → sudden 10/10
  • Calm → restless
  • Normal speech → slurred speech
  • Warm extremity → cool and pale
A sudden or unexpected change is often more important than a chronic finding.
6. Baseline Matters

Do not evaluate a lab result, vital sign, or symptom without considering the client's baseline.

Example: A client with COPD normally has an oxygen saturation of 88%–92%. Current SpO₂: 90%. Although 90% may appear low compared with a healthy adult, it is within this client's usual baseline.

Now compare: Baseline 90% → Current 78% with confusion and cyanosis. This IS a significant change.

NCLEX Rule: Compare the client with the client's own baseline whenever baseline information is provided.
7. Stable vs. Unstable

Stable Findings

  • Chronic findings
  • Expected findings
  • Predictable symptoms
  • Normal postoperative findings
  • Improving symptoms
  • No significant change from baseline

Unstable Findings

  • Acute changes / unexpected findings
  • Respiratory distress
  • New neurological changes
  • Significant bleeding
  • Sudden hypotension / new severe chest pain
  • New airway problems
  • Change in level of consciousness
  • Rapid clinical deterioration
A client with a chronic abnormal finding may be more stable than a client with a new change.
8. Expected vs. Unexpected

Not every abnormal-looking finding is an emergency. Ask: "Is this finding expected for this client's condition, treatment, or stage of recovery?"

Example: A client is 12 hours postoperative following abdominal surgery and reports incisional pain — some postoperative pain is expected. Now compare: a postoperative client suddenly develops confusion, restlessness, shortness of breath, and falling oxygen saturation — these findings are unexpected and require more attention.

Important Principle: Expected does not automatically mean normal for a healthy person. It means expected for this specific situation.
9. Identify the Question Category

Assessment Questions

Clues: "Which finding...", "Which assessment...", "Which manifestation...", "Which finding requires follow-up/should be reported?"

Think: What am I looking for in the client?

Intervention Questions

Clues: "Which action should the nurse take?", "Which intervention is appropriate?", "What should the nurse do first?"

Think: What should the nurse do?

Teaching Questions

Clues: "Indicates understanding", "Requires further teaching", "Which instruction/statement should the nurse include?"

Think: What should the client correctly know or do?

Priority Questions

Clues: "First", "Priority", "Immediate", "Highest priority", "Most important", "See/assess first"

Think: Which problem cannot safely wait?

Evaluation Questions

Clues: "Which finding indicates treatment was effective?", "Which outcome demonstrates improvement?"

Think: Did the intervention work?

Safety Questions

Clues: "Which prescription should the nurse question?", "Which action requires intervention?", "Which action is contraindicated?"

Think: What could harm the client?

10. Pay Attention to Key Words
One word can completely change the correct answer.
Key WordMeaning
FIRSTWhich action happens before the others? Several choices may eventually be correct — only one comes first.
IMMEDIATEWhat cannot safely wait?
EXPECTEDWhat is anticipated in this situation?
UNEXPECTEDWhat is abnormal or concerning?
FURTHER TEACHINGFind the incorrect statement.
INDICATES UNDERSTANDINGFind the correct statement.
11. Do Not Add Information

Use only the information provided in the question. Do not create imaginary information.

Example: A client refuses a medication. Do not assume "Maybe the client cannot afford it," "Maybe the client is allergic," or "Maybe the client does not speak English." None of these facts were provided.

NCLEX Rule: Never create additional information to make an answer correct. If your reasoning begins with "Maybe...", ask yourself: "Where does the question say that?" If the question does not say it, do not use it.
12. Do Not Diagnose From One Abnormal Finding

One abnormal vital sign or lab value does not automatically establish an entire diagnosis.

Example: Heart rate 112/min — does this automatically mean shock? No. Other findings must be considered: blood pressure, mental status, respiratory status, skin, urine output, bleeding, symptoms, medical history.

NCLEX Rule: Look for patterns of clinical cues instead of making a diagnosis from one isolated finding.
13. Predict the Answer Before Studying the Options

After understanding the question, think about what type of answer you expect. You may not know the exact answer yet — that is okay. Predict the general direction.

Example: A postoperative client suddenly develops shortness of breath, tachycardia, and SpO₂ 82%. Before examining the answer choices, think: "This is an acute oxygenation problem. I probably need an immediate respiratory intervention." Now the answer choices become easier to evaluate.

14. Do Not Let the Answer Choices Control Your Thinking

Answer choices are designed to distract you. Some distractors may be true nursing interventions, appropriate later, related to the disease, commonly used treatments, or things you remember from studying. But a true statement can still be the wrong NCLEX answer.

The important question is: Does this answer actually answer what was asked?

15. A True Answer Can Still Be Wrong

A client experiencing hypoglycemia is awake and able to swallow. Which action should the nurse take first? Possible actions: treat the hypoglycemia, notify the provider, teach prevention, document the event. All may eventually occur — but the question asks FIRST. The immediate problem must be treated before teaching and documentation.

NCLEX Rule: Do not ask only "Is this answer true?" Also ask "Is this the answer to THIS question?"
16. Keep the Answer in the Same Category as the Question
  • Assessment → look for assessment information
  • Intervention → look for what the nurse should do
  • Teaching outcome → look for correct client knowledge or behavior
  • Evaluation → look for improvement or response to treatment
  • Safety problem → look for the dangerous or inappropriate action

An answer may be medically related to the topic but still belong to the wrong category.

17. Eliminate Distractors
Before selecting an answer, ask three questions: 1) Is it correct? 2) Is it safe? 3) Does it answer the question being asked? If the answer fails one of these tests, eliminate it.

Common Distractors

  • Unsafe or irrelevant answers
  • Outside nursing scope
  • Correct but not needed now
  • Related to the diagnosis but not the question
  • Based on information that was never provided
  • Appropriate later but not first
18. Be Careful With Absolute Words

Pay attention to words such as always, never, only, all, completely. These words do not automatically make an answer incorrect — however, an absolute statement must truly be correct in the entire situation described.

Rule: Absolute wording should make you examine the answer more carefully.
19. Do Not Choose an Answer Because It Sounds More Advanced

The most complicated answer is not automatically the correct answer. NCLEX frequently favors safety, basic nursing actions, appropriate nursing scope, direct interventions, client-centered care, and the least invasive appropriate action. A simple nursing action can be more correct than a complicated medical intervention.

20. Time Changes the Meaning of Findings

Always notice timing: 30 minutes after surgery · 2 hours after surgery · postoperative day 1 · three days after surgery · immediately after medication · one hour after medication · newly started medication · several weeks after treatment.

Example: Incisional pain shortly after surgery may be expected. Sudden severe pain several days after surgery accompanied by fever, rigidity, and hypotension is very different.

NCLEX Rule: Always interpret findings according to when they occurred.
21. Recognize Change of Condition

A change from baseline is one of the strongest NCLEX clues.

  • Before: alert and oriented → Now: confused and restless
  • Before: BP 126/78 mm Hg → Now: BP 84/50 mm Hg
  • Before: SpO₂ 97% → Now: SpO₂ 86%
  • Before: urine output 50 mL/hr → Now: 10 mL/hr
The question is often not "Is this number abnormal?" It is "What happened to this client?"
22. The Final Two Answers

Many NCLEX questions eventually come down to two choices that both seem reasonable. When this happens, do not simply say "Both are correct." Compare them carefully. Ask:

  • Which answer addresses the exact question?
  • Which answer is safer?
  • Which answer applies to this client right now?
  • Which answer uses only the information provided?
  • Is one answer appropriate later but not now?
  • Am I inventing information to make one answer correct?
  • Is one answer medically true but unrelated to the task?
The goal is often not "the only correct statement" — it is the best answer for this specific situation.
23. Complete NCLEX Question Method
1
WHO? Who is the client? (age, diagnosis, procedure, pregnancy, postoperative status, relevant history)
2
WHAT IS HAPPENING? What is the current clinical situation?
3
WHAT CHANGED? Is there a new or sudden finding?
4
EXPECTED OR UNEXPECTED? Is the finding normal for this situation or concerning?
5
WHAT IS THE TASK? What exactly is the question asking?
6
FIND THE KEY WORD. First? Priority? Immediate? Expected? Unexpected? Further teaching? Follow-up?
7
PREDICT. What type of answer should you expect?
8
ELIMINATE. Remove answers that are unsafe, irrelevant, based on assumptions, outside the question, or correct but lower priority.
9
COMPARE THE FINAL TWO. Which answer best fits this client, this situation, and this moment?
24. W-C-T-P-E Method

A simple way to remember the process:

  • W — WHO? Who is the client?
  • C — CHANGE. What changed?
  • T — TASK. What exactly are they asking?
  • P — PREDICT. What kind of answer should I expect?
  • E — ELIMINATE. Remove the distractors.
25. Practice Examples

Example 1 — Further Teaching

A nurse is providing discharge teaching to a client taking warfarin. Which statement by the client indicates a need for further teaching?

  • A. "I will tell my dentist that I take warfarin."
  • B. "I will use a soft-bristled toothbrush."
  • C. "I will take aspirin when I have a headache." ✅ Correct Answer
  • D. "I will report unusual bleeding."

Strategy: "Need for further teaching" = find the incorrect statement. Aspirin can increase bleeding risk with anticoagulant therapy unless specifically directed. The biggest strategy point is not remembering warfarin — it is recognizing the question asks for the WRONG statement.

Example 2 — Immediate Intervention

A nurse assesses a client 2 hours after thyroid surgery. Which finding requires immediate intervention?

  • A. Incisional pain rated 4/10
  • B. Mild sore throat
  • C. Stridor ✅ Correct Answer
  • D. Small amount of drainage on the dressing

Strategy: Stridor indicates possible upper airway obstruction. The other findings may occur after surgery. The question asks: which finding cannot wait?

Example 3 — Baseline

A client with COPD has a usual oxygen saturation of 88%–92%. The current oxygen saturation is 90%. What should the nurse do?

  • A. Activate the rapid response team
  • B. Continue monitoring ✅ Correct Answer
  • C. Prepare the client for intubation
  • D. Notify the provider immediately

Strategy: The current value is within the client's baseline. Compare current condition vs. baseline, not just against a healthy adult.

Example 4 — Change From Baseline

A client's BP was 126/78 mm Hg 1 hour ago. Current BP is 84/50 mm Hg, and the client reports dizziness. What information is most important?

  • A. The client is hospitalized
  • B. The client reports dizziness
  • C. The client has had a significant change from baseline ✅ Correct Answer
  • D. The blood pressure was measured 1 hour ago

Strategy: The major clue is the change 126/78 → 84/50. NCLEX frequently tests whether the nurse recognizes deterioration from baseline.

Example 5 — Task Changes, Topic Doesn't

A nurse is teaching a client about a prescribed medication. Which statement indicates understanding? → Translation: find the correct statement.

Now change only the question: Which statement requires follow-up? → Translation: find the incorrect or concerning statement.

Strategy: The medical topic did not change — the task changed.

Example 6 — Predicting the Answer

A client receiving an IV medication suddenly develops difficulty breathing and facial swelling. Which action should the nurse take first?

Prediction: This may represent a severe allergic reaction affecting the airway — the expected answer should involve an immediate safety intervention.

Example 7 — Chronic vs. Acute

A client has experienced chronic back pain for 5 years. Another client suddenly develops severe chest pressure and diaphoresis. Which client is more concerning?

Answer: The client with sudden chest pressure and diaphoresis — an acute, potentially life-threatening change outranks a chronic problem.

Example 8 — Unexpected Mental Status Change

A postoperative client reports incisional pain 6/10 with stable vital signs. Another client who was previously alert suddenly becomes confused and restless. Which client requires more immediate assessment?

Answer: The client with the sudden mental-status change. Postoperative pain may be expected; sudden confusion is an unexpected change.

Example 9 — Do Not Assume

A client says, "I don't want to take this medication." What should the nurse do?

Strategy: Do not assume cost problems, allergy, or language barrier. The nurse should first determine why the client does not want the medication — use only the information given.

Example 10 — One Cue Is Not a Diagnosis

A client has a heart rate of 112/min. Can the nurse automatically conclude the client is in shock?

Answer: No. Tachycardia may occur for many reasons. Look for additional cues: hypotension, altered mental status, decreased urine output, cool skin, bleeding, respiratory changes. One cue alone does not establish the whole condition.

26. Common NCLEX Reading Mistakes
  • Mistake 1 — Answering the disease instead of the question. Correction: identify the task before choosing an answer.
  • Mistake 2 — Missing a key word (first, immediate, further teaching, expected, unexpected). Correction: identify the key word before evaluating answers.
  • Mistake 3 — Adding information ("Maybe the client..."). Correction: use only what was provided.
  • Mistake 4 — Choosing a true but irrelevant answer. Correction: ask "Does this answer what they actually asked?"
  • Mistake 5 — Ignoring baseline. Correction: compare current findings vs. usual findings.
  • Mistake 6 — Ignoring a sudden change. Correction: notice "suddenly, newly, now, developed, became, increased, decreased."
  • Mistake 7 — Diagnosing from one finding. Correction: look for a pattern of cues.
  • Mistake 8 — Overthinking. The question gives enough information to choose an answer — do not create a more complicated situation than the one provided.

28. Question Error Analysis

When an NCLEX question is answered incorrectly, do not only memorize the correct answer. Determine why the question was missed.

Possible Reasons

  • Knowledge deficit
  • Misread the stem / missed a key word
  • Missed "further teaching"
  • Added information
  • Ignored baseline / missed an acute change
  • Chose a true but irrelevant answer
  • Overthought the question
  • Did not recognize expected vs. unexpected
  • Changed the correct answer
  • Misidentified the question category

For every incorrect question, identify: 1) What was the question asking? 2) What clue did I miss? 3) Why was my answer wrong? 4) What rule will prevent this mistake next time?

30. Final NCLEX Question Check
  • WHO? Who is my client?
  • WHAT? What is happening?
  • CHANGE? What changed?
  • BASELINE? Is this normal for this client?
  • EXPECTED? Expected or unexpected?
  • TASK? What exactly are they asking?
  • KEY WORD? First? Immediate? Further teaching? Follow-up?
  • PREDICT? What kind of answer should I expect?
  • ELIMINATE? Which answers are unsafe, irrelevant, or based on assumptions?
  • FINAL TWO? Which answer best applies to this client right now?
Key Rules to Remember
  • Do not answer until you understand what is being asked.
  • Identify the key word.
  • "Further teaching" means look for the incorrect statement.
  • "Indicates understanding" means look for the correct statement.
  • "Requires follow-up" means something may be wrong or concerning.
  • Expected findings are not automatically emergencies.
  • Compare abnormal findings with the client's baseline.
  • A new change is often more important than a chronic finding.
  • Use only the information provided — do not create "maybe" scenarios.
  • One abnormal finding does not automatically establish a diagnosis.
  • Predict the type of answer before analyzing the choices.
  • A true answer can still be the wrong NCLEX answer.
  • Ask whether the answer actually answers the question.
  • When stuck between two answers, compare safety, timing, relevance, and the client's current condition.
🧠 Final Rule: Do not answer the disease. Answer the question. Before every NCLEX answer, think: Who is my client? What changed? What are they asking me? What type of answer am I looking for? Why are the other answers wrong?
Sample Question 1 — Further Teaching

A nurse is teaching a client with heart failure about home care. Which statement by the client requires further teaching?

  • A. "I will weigh myself every morning after I urinate."
  • B. "I will notify my provider if I gain 3 lb (1.4 kg) in 2 days."
  • C. "I will stop taking my diuretic when my ankle swelling improves." ✅ Correct Answer
  • D. "I will limit sodium as instructed by my healthcare provider."

What is this REALLY asking? Do not immediately think "what do I know about heart failure?" — first identify the task. "Requires further teaching" = find the incorrect statement (a negative question). A, B, and D demonstrate appropriate understanding. C is incorrect because prescribed medications should not be independently stopped when symptoms improve.

Teaching Point: A student may see three correct statements and become confused because all three "sound right." That is exactly the point — you are looking for the WRONG statement.

NCLEX Rule: Further teaching = find the incorrect answer.

Sample Question 2 — Baseline

A client with COPD has a usual SpO₂ of 89%–92%. During the morning assessment, the nurse notes an SpO₂ of 90%. The client is awake, talking comfortably, and denies shortness of breath. Which action should the nurse take?

  • A. Activate the rapid response team
  • B. Apply 15 L/min oxygen by nonrebreather mask
  • C. Continue to monitor the client ✅ Correct Answer
  • D. Notify the healthcare provider immediately

The trap: A student may see SpO₂ 90% and think "that is low!" But NCLEX gave you something more important — usual saturation 89%–92%. This client has not changed from baseline.

W-C-T-P-E: WHO = client with COPD. CHANGE = no significant change. TASK = what should the nurse do? PREDICT = if stable and at baseline, probably continue routine care. ELIMINATE = rapid response, emergency oxygen, and immediate provider notification are excessive based on the information provided.

NCLEX Rule: When baseline is provided, compare the client with their own baseline.

Sample Question 3 — Look for the Change

The nurse receives report on four clients. Which client should the nurse assess first?

  • A. A client with chronic arthritis reporting knee pain of 5/10
  • B. A client with pneumonia whose temperature remains 38.1°C (100.6°F)
  • C. A client who was alert 30 minutes ago and is now confused and restless ✅ Correct Answer
  • D. A client with chronic kidney disease whose creatinine remains elevated

Key word: FIRST = which client cannot safely wait? A = chronic problem. B = consistent with pneumonia. C = acute neurological/mental-status change. D = chronic abnormality. The word that matters most is NOW — was alert → now confused and restless represents a change in condition.

Teaching Point: Students often choose the answer with the "worst disease." The client with chronic kidney disease may be seriously ill, but the question is: who needs attention right now?

NCLEX Rule: Acute/unexpected change usually outranks a chronic, expected, or unchanged finding.

Sample Question 4 — Expected vs. Unexpected

A nurse assesses a client 6 hours after abdominal surgery. Which finding requires follow-up?

  • A. Incisional pain rated 4/10
  • B. Mild drowsiness after receiving an opioid analgesic
  • C. Small amount of serosanguineous drainage on the dressing
  • D. New restlessness with an oxygen saturation of 84% ✅ Correct Answer

Key phrase: "Requires follow-up" = find the concerning finding. Some pain, mild drowsiness after pain medication, and a small amount of postoperative drainage may be anticipated at 6 hours postop. New restlessness + SpO₂ 84% is unexpected and potentially indicates inadequate oxygenation.

NCLEX Rule: Expected does not mean normal for every healthy adult — it means anticipated in this client's specific situation.

Sample Question 5 — True but Not First

A client with diabetes mellitus is awake and able to swallow. Blood glucose is 48 mg/dL. Which action should the nurse take first?

  • A. Give 15 g of a rapid-acting carbohydrate ✅ Correct Answer
  • B. Notify the healthcare provider
  • C. Teach the client how to prevent future hypoglycemia
  • D. Document the hypoglycemic episode

The trap: Are B, C, and D ridiculous? No — they may all occur eventually. But the question contains one word: FIRST. The immediate problem is hypoglycemia — treat it first, then reassess and perform later actions as indicated.

NCLEX Rule: Correct later ≠ correct now.

Sample Question 6 — Do Not Add Information

A client tells the nurse, "I don't want to take this medication." Which response by the nurse is most appropriate?

  • A. "Are you unable to afford the medication?"
  • B. "Are you worried because you had an allergic reaction before?"
  • C. "Tell me what concerns you about taking the medication." ✅ Correct Answer
  • D. "I can ask the provider to prescribe a different medication."

What do we actually know? Only that the client does not want the medication. A assumes financial difficulty, B assumes a previous allergy, D assumes the medication needs to change. C obtains more information without inventing a reason.

NCLEX Rule: If your reasoning starts with "Maybe...", ask "Where does the question say that?" Use only the information provided.

Sample Question 7 — One Finding Is Not a Diagnosis

The nurse assesses a client whose heart rate is 116/min. Which action is most appropriate?

  • A. Document that the client is developing shock
  • B. Immediately prepare the client for emergency intubation
  • C. Assess blood pressure, mental status, respiratory status, and other clinical findings ✅ Correct Answer
  • D. Inform the client that the tachycardia confirms internal bleeding

Teaching Point: NCLEX frequently gives one abnormal piece of information and waits to see whether you build an entire diagnosis around it. Don't jump: tachycardia → shock → hemorrhage → emergency. Instead, look for a pattern of cues.

NCLEX Rule: One abnormal finding does not automatically establish an entire diagnosis.

Sample Question 8 — Answer the Category Asked

A client with pneumonia has received prescribed antibiotics for 48 hours. Which finding best indicates that the treatment has been effective?

  • A. The nurse teaches the client to cough and deep breathe
  • B. The client's temperature decreases from 39.2°C (102.6°F) to 37.5°C (99.5°F) ✅ Correct Answer
  • C. The nurse obtains a sputum specimen
  • D. The healthcare provider prescribes another chest x-ray

Identify the category: This is an EVALUATION question — translation: did the treatment work? A is an intervention, C is an assessment/diagnostic action, D is a medical prescription. B gives an outcome showing improvement.

NCLEX Rule: Keep the answer in the same category as the question.

Sample Question 9 — Timing Changes Everything

Client 1: One hour after abdominal surgery reports incisional pain of 6/10, stable vitals. Client 2: Three days after abdominal surgery suddenly develops severe abdominal pain, rigidity, fever, and hypotension. Which client should the nurse assess first?

  • A. Client 1 because postoperative pain should always be treated immediately
  • B. Client 1 because the surgery occurred more recently
  • C. Client 2 because the findings represent an unexpected change ✅ Correct Answer
  • D. Client 2 because all postoperative clients develop complications after 3 days

Strategy: Pain one hour after surgery may be expected. Client 2's sudden severe pain, rigidity, fever, and hypotension three days out is a very different pattern. Also notice D uses the absolute word "ALL" — not supported.

NCLEX Rule: Always interpret findings according to what happened + when it happened.

Sample Question 10 — The Final Two Answers

A nurse is caring for a client receiving an IV antibiotic. Ten minutes after the infusion begins, the client suddenly develops facial swelling, wheezing, and difficulty breathing. Which action should the nurse take first?

  • A. Stop the IV antibiotic infusion ✅ Correct Answer
  • B. Document the client's reaction
  • C. Ask the client whether this has happened before
  • D. Provide teaching about medication allergies

W-C-T-P-E: WHO = client receiving IV antibiotic. CHANGE = sudden facial swelling + wheezing + difficulty breathing. TASK = what should the nurse do FIRST? PREDICT = this is an acute airway/safety problem — expect an immediate action that stops further exposure. B, C, and D may eventually happen, but none addresses the immediate danger. A does — the dangerous exposure is still continuing.

Final-Two Rule: Which answers the exact question? Which is safest? Which applies right now? Which can safely wait? Am I adding information? Is one merely true but lower priority?

NCLEX Rule: The goal is not always the only medically true statement — it is the best answer for this client, in this situation, at this moment.

🧠 NCLEX Memory Line: Do not answer the disease. Answer the question.

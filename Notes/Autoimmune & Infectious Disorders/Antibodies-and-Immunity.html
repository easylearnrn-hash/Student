Antibodies and Immunity
GAMED + 4 Types of Immunity + NCLEX Traps

High-yield NCLEX review: immunoglobulins, active vs passive immunity, and classic exam pitfalls.

A. Definition

GAMED is an easy memory trick used to remember the five major immunoglobulins (antibodies made by the immune system to recognize and help fight foreign substances).

The five antibodies are:

  • G=IgG
    Long-term protection, placenta
  • A=IgA
    Mucosa and body secretions
  • M=IgM
    First response in new infection
  • E=IgE
    Allergy, anaphylaxis, parasites
  • D=IgD
    B-cell antigen receptor

How do antibodies work?

An antibody (a protective protein made by the immune system) is designed to recognize a specific antigen (a foreign substance or marker that the immune system recognizes).

Antigens may be found on:

  • Bacteria (microorganisms that can cause infection)
  • Viruses (infectious particles that enter and reproduce inside cells)
  • Parasites (organisms that live in or on another organism)
  • Toxins (harmful substances)
  • Allergens (substances that can trigger an allergic reaction)

Think of an antibody like a lock and key

The tip of an antibody contains an antigen-binding site (the part of the antibody that attaches to a specific antigen).

Each antibody is made to recognize a particular antigen.

Correct antibody + matching antigen -> they bind together.

For example:

Bacterium enters the body
-> the bacterium has an antigen on its surface
-> the correct antibody recognizes that antigen
-> the antibody binds (attaches) to it
-> the immune system can now more easily identify and destroy the bacterium.

What happens after the antibody binds?

Depending on the antibody, binding can:

  • Block the pathogen so it cannot enter or damage cells.
  • Mark the pathogen for destruction so immune cells can find it more easily.
  • Cause several pathogens to clump together, making them easier to remove.
  • Activate other parts of the immune system to help destroy the pathogen.

Why are there 5 different antibodies?

The five antibodies perform different jobs and work in different places.

For example:

  • IgG -> protects the blood and tissues and can cross the placenta.
  • IgA -> protects body secretions and mucosal surfaces (moist linings such as the mouth, intestines, and respiratory tract).
  • IgM -> is usually the first antibody produced during a new infection.
  • IgE -> works with allergies, anaphylaxis (severe life-threatening allergic reaction), and parasites.
  • IgD -> helps B cells recognize antigens.
TIP
Easy NCLEX Concept

Antigen = the target.
Antibody = the protein that recognizes the target.
Binding = antibody attaches to the matching antigen.
After binding = the immune system can block, mark, clump, or destroy the threat.

GAMED = 5 different antibodies -> 5 different specialized jobs.

C. Four Types of Immunity

1. Natural Active

Patient gets an infection naturally, then their own immune system makes antibodies and memory cells.

Example: Chickenpox infection -> immune response -> memory forms.

Think: Get the disease -> Natural Active

2. Artificial Active

Patient receives a vaccine that stimulates their own immune system to make antibodies and memory cells.

Think: Vaccine -> Artificial Active

3. Natural Passive

Ready-made antibodies are naturally transferred from mother to baby.

  • Placenta -> IgG
  • Breast milk -> IgA

Think: Mom gives antibodies -> Natural Passive

4. Artificial Passive

Ready-made antibodies are medically given for immediate temporary protection.

Examples: Rabies immune globulin, HBIG, TIG, antivenom.

Think: Injection of antibodies -> Artificial Passive

E. Nursing Priorities

!
Severe Allergic Reaction / Anaphylaxis

IgE can trigger mast-cell activation, airway swelling, and bronchoconstriction. Prioritize Airway, Breathing, Circulation. IM epinephrine is first-line treatment for anaphylaxis.

F. Safety and Infection Control

  • Monitor after vaccines and new medications for allergic reactions.
  • Watch for wheezing, facial swelling, stridor, and hypotension.
  • Stridor (high-pitched sound from upper-airway narrowing) = airway emergency.
  • Follow hand hygiene and infection-prevention precautions.

G. Special Populations

Newborn
  • Before birth: receives IgG through placenta.
  • After birth via breastfeeding: receives IgA.

H. NCLEX Traps and Tricks

Trap 1
First antibody produced after a new infection?
IgM — not IgG
Trap 2
Most important antibody on repeat exposure?
IgG
First exposure → IgM  ·  Second exposure → IgG
Trap 3
Which antibody crosses the placenta?
IgG — not IgA
Placenta = IgG  ·  Breast milk = IgA
Trap 4
Which antibody is found in breast milk?
IgA — not IgG
Trap 5
Patient receives a vaccine. Type of immunity?
Artificial Active
Artificial = given intentionally  ·  Active = body makes antibodies
Trap 6
Patient receives rabies immune globulin after exposure.
Artificial Passive
Antibodies are already made and medically provided
Trap 7
Patient recovered from infection and is now immune.
Natural Active
Infection happened naturally and patient's own body made antibodies
Trap 8
Baby receives antibodies from mother through placenta.
Natural Passive
Baby did not make the antibodies — they were passed from mom
Trap 9
Which type of immunity provides immediate protection?
Passive immunity
Antibodies already exist — no wait for production
Trap 10
Which immunity produces memory cells?
Active immunity
Passive immunity does not create immune memory
Trap 11
Patient needs immediate antibody protection after exposure.
Immune globulin — not vaccine alone
Passive = immediate  ·  Vaccine = slower, long-term
Trap 12
Patient receives both vaccine and immune globulin. Same job?
No — different roles
Immune globulin = immediate temporary  ·  Vaccine = slower long-term
Trap 13
Immunoglobulin linked to anaphylaxis?
IgE
IgE → mast-cell activation → anaphylaxis → epinephrine
Trap 14
Which antibody protects mucosal surfaces?
IgA
Tears, saliva, breast milk, respiratory and GI secretions
Trap 15
Classic B-cell receptor NCLEX answer?
IgD
IgM can also exist on B cells, but IgD = NCLEX standard answer
Trap 16
Which antibody is produced in the largest amount in the body overall?
IgG
IgG = most abundant immunoglobulin in blood and tissues

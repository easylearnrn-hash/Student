Burns and Skin

Skin Conditions Overview

Common skin conditions, causes, treatments, skin cancer detection, and NCLEX priorities

1. What Is a Skin Condition?

Any disorder affecting the skin, hair, or nails. Some are temporary (rashes); others are chronic (long-term).

Common Causes
  • Infections — bacterial, viral, fungal, or parasitic
  • Inflammation — allergic reactions, autoimmune disease
  • Environmental factors — sun exposure, irritants, humidity

3. Treatment by Category

A. Bacterial (Impetigo, Cellulitis, Folliculitis)
  • Antibiotic ointments (mupirocin, bacitracin) for minor cases
  • Oral antibiotics (cephalexin, clindamycin) for severe cases
  • Keep affected areas clean & avoid touching lesions
B. Viral (Shingles, Warts, Herpes Simplex)
  • Antiviral medications (acyclovir, valacyclovir) for shingles & herpes
  • Pain management (NSAIDs, lidocaine cream) for shingles
  • Shingles vaccine (zoster vaccine) for prevention in adults >50
C. Fungal (Ringworm, Athlete's Foot, Candidiasis)
  • Antifungal creams (clotrimazole, miconazole) for mild cases
  • Oral antifungals (fluconazole, terbinafine) for widespread infections
  • Keep skin dry & wear loose clothing — fungus thrives in moisture
D. Parasitic (Scabies, Lice)
  • Permethrin cream kills scabies mites
  • Wash clothes & bedding in hot water
  • Treat all household members to prevent reinfection
E. Chronic (Eczema, Psoriasis, Vitiligo, Melasma)
  • Moisturizers to prevent dryness & itching
  • Topical steroids (hydrocortisone, triamcinolone) to reduce inflammation
  • Phototherapy (light therapy) for vitiligo & psoriasis
  • Sunscreen — prevents worsening of melasma & vitiligo

4. Skin Cancer (Malignant Skin Conditions)

🔤
ABCDE Rule for Skin Cancer Detection

Asymmetry — one side looks different. Border irregularity — uneven edges. Color changes — multiple colors in one mole. Diameter >6mm. Evolving — growing, itching, bleeding, or changing.

TypeCauseAppearanceRisk Factors
Basal Cell Carcinoma (BCC)Sun exposure, UV radiationWaxy bump, pearly border, slow-growingFair skin, sunburns, long-term sun exposure
Squamous Cell Carcinoma (SCC)Sun exposure, chronic woundsScaly, red patch or ulcer; can spreadFair skin, sun exposure, HPV infection
MelanomaGenetic + UV radiationIrregular dark mole, changing size/color/shapeFair skin, tanning beds, many moles, family history
Treatment
  • Surgical removal (excision, Mohs surgery)
  • Chemotherapy or radiation for advanced cases
  • Sunscreen & avoiding tanning beds for prevention

5. NCLEX Priorities

  • Teach patients to avoid scratching — prevents infection
  • Use hypoallergenic soaps & moisturizers for sensitive skin
  • Fungal infections — keep skin dry & avoid sharing personal items
  • Bacterial infections — wash hands & cover sores to prevent spread
  • Shingles — avoid contact with pregnant women & immunocompromised people
📝
NCLEX "Must-Know" Concepts

Shingles = painful blistering rash → acyclovir + pain meds. Ringworm = circular red rash → antifungal cream. Scabies = itchy rash, worse at night → permethrin cream. Melanoma = irregular dark mole → urgent dermatology referral. Psoriasis = silvery scaly patches → steroids + UV therapy. Eczema = itchy dry skin → moisturize + avoid triggers.

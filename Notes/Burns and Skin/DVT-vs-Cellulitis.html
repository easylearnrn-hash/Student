DVT vs. Cellulitis

Telling apart two "red, swollen leg" presentations — and their very different emergencies

Side-by-Side Comparison

FeatureDeep Vein Thrombosis (DVT)Cellulitis
DefinitionBlood clot in a deep vein, usually lower extremitiesAcute bacterial skin & subcutaneous tissue infection
EtiologyVenous stasis, hypercoagulability, endothelial injury (Virchow's triad)Streptococcus or Staphylococcus via skin break/wound
Risk FactorsRecent surgery/trauma, prolonged immobility, obesity, smoking, oral contraceptives, cancerDiabetes, skin injury, immunosuppression, lymphedema
LocationCommonly calf or thigh (deep veins)Skin & soft tissue, usually unilateral leg or arm
PainDull, aching, cramping — worse with walking/standingLocalized tenderness or burning sensation
SwellingUnilateral leg swelling, pitting edemaLocalized swelling, not usually pitting
Redness/WarmthMild redness over vein path, warm to touchBright red, hot, and shiny skin
Skin AppearanceMay look normal or slightly duskyShiny, tight, red, warm — possible blisters/drainage
Systemic SignsPossible low-grade fever; risk of PE (dyspnea, chest pain)Fever, malaise, chills common
Key AssessmentHoman's sign (unreliable), duplex ultrasoundMark area with pen to monitor progression
Labs/ImagingD-dimer (↑), venous Doppler ultrasoundCBC (↑ WBCs), blood cultures if febrile/severe
TreatmentAnticoagulation (heparin, warfarin); elevation, compression stockings (not acute phase)Antibiotics (oral or IV); warm compresses; pain control
Nursing PrioritiesMonitor for PE; avoid massage; encourage ambulation if stableMonitor spread of infection; elevate limb; educate on wound care

NCLEX Keyword Recognition

DVT Key Words

"Unilateral leg swelling," "pain with walking," "post-op," "immobile," "anticoagulants," "PE symptoms"

Cellulitis Key Words

"Red hot swollen skin," "localized," "bacterial infection," "antibiotics," "fever," "wound or insect bite"

🚨
DVT → Pulmonary Embolism Warning Signs (Test Favorite!)

Sudden chest pain, shortness of breath, hemoptysis (coughing blood), decreased O₂ saturation. This is an immediate emergency — call the provider and prepare for oxygen, imaging, and possible anticoagulation.

NCLEX Red FlagDVTCellulitis
Think...Sudden shortness of breath or chest pain → PERapidly spreading redness, high fever → sepsis risk
📝
NCLEX Summary

DVT = clot, dull ache, unilateral pitting edema, risk of PE — anticoagulate, avoid massage. Cellulitis = bacterial infection, hot/shiny/bright red skin, fever — antibiotics, mark borders to track spread, elevate. When in doubt, sudden respiratory symptoms point to DVT/PE; spreading redness with fever points to worsening cellulitis/sepsis.

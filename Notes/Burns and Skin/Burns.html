Burns and Skin

Burns

Classification, TBSA, fluid resuscitation, airway risk, phases of care, and complications

What Is a Burn?

A burn is damage to the skin and tissues caused by heat, chemicals, electricity, or radiation (like sunlight or X-rays). Severity depends on:

  • Depth — how deep the burn is
  • TBSA — how much skin is burned (Total Body Surface Area)
  • Location — face, hands, feet, genitals, airway = more serious

Moist/pink = 2nd degree. Leathery = 3rd degree. Bone exposed = 4th degree.

TBSA — Total Body Surface Area (Rule of Nines)

Used to estimate how much of the body is burned, for fluid resuscitation and classification.

Adults
  • Head & neck: 9% (4.5% anterior, 4.5% posterior)
  • Anterior trunk: 18%
  • Posterior trunk: 18%
  • Each arm (total): 9% (4.5% anterior, 4.5% posterior)
  • Each leg (total): 18% (9% anterior, 9% posterior)
  • Perineum/genitals: 1%
Children (Adjusted)
  • Head & neck: 18% (9% anterior, 9% posterior)
  • Anterior trunk: 18%
  • Posterior trunk: 18%
  • Each arm (total): 9% (4.5% anterior, 4.5% posterior)
  • Each leg (total): 14% (7% anterior, 7% posterior)
  • Perineum/genitals: 1%

Fluid Resuscitation — Parkland Formula

Used in the emergent phase to prevent shock.

  • Adults: 4 mL × %TBSA × kg
  • Children: 3 mL × %TBSA × kg
  • Give ½ in the first 8 hours, then ½ over the next 16 hours
  • Use Lactated Ringer's — replaces fluids/electrolytes; only under supervision

Airway Burns (Inhalation Injury) — Life-Threatening 🚨

Caused by breathing in smoke, steam, or toxic gases (carbon monoxide or cyanide).

🚨
Signs of a Dangerous Airway Burn

Soot around mouth/nose, singed nasal hairs, hoarseness, stridor (airway closing), confusion or cherry-red skin (CO poisoning). Intubate early — before swelling closes the airway!

Burn Treatment: 3 Phases of Care

A. Emergent Phase (First 24–48 Hours) — Save the Life

Priority: Airway, Fluids, Prevent Shock

  • Check airway — intubate if inhalation injury suspected
  • Fluid replacement — Parkland formula IV fluids
  • Pain control — IV opioids (morphine, fentanyl)
  • Cover with a clean, dry sheet — prevents infection & heat loss
B. Acute Phase (48 Hours – Weeks) — Heal the Wound

Priority: Wound Healing, Infection Prevention, Nutrition

  • Prevent infection — sterile wound care, antibiotic ointments (silver sulfadiazine)
  • Debridement (removing dead tissue) — may need skin grafts
  • High-calorie, high-protein diet — burns increase metabolism
C. Rehabilitation Phase (Months – Years) — Restore Function

Priority: Physical Therapy, Scar Prevention, Emotional Support

  • Range of motion (ROM) exercises to prevent contractures
  • Compression garments to reduce scars
  • Support groups — burn survivors often have emotional trauma

Special Types of Burns

⚡ Electrical Burns

Hidden damage — can injure heart, brain, muscles, organs.

  • Monitor heart (risk for deadly arrhythmias like V-fib)
  • Check urine for myoglobin (rhabdomyolysis — muscle breakdown that can damage kidneys)
  • Give high IV fluids to flush kidneys
🧪 Chemical Burns

Can keep burning until fully washed off.

  • Remove contaminated clothing
  • Flush skin with water for at least 20–30 minutes
  • If a dry powder chemical, brush it off first, then rinse

Complications to Watch For

⚠️
Hypovolemic Shock (First 24–48 Hours)

Signs: low BP, fast HR, pale/cold skin. Treat with IV fluids (Parkland formula).

⚠️
Compartment Syndrome

Too much swelling inside tight skin blocks blood flow. Signs: pain, pale skin, no pulse, tingling, paralysis. Treated with escharotomy (cutting burned skin to release pressure).

⚠️
Sepsis

Life-threatening infection in burn patients. Signs: fever, fast HR, low BP, confusion. Treat with IV antibiotics and sterile wound care.

Why Not IM or PO Meds Early?

In the emergent burn phase, circulation is poor — especially to muscles and the gut.

  • IM (intramuscular) — absorbed slowly into the bloodstream (e.g., flu shot)
  • PO (per os, "by mouth") — absorbed through the digestive system
💡
Why IV Only?

IM and PO meds may not absorb properly when circulation is poor. IV (intravenous) meds are the safest and fastest route in the emergent phase.

NCLEX Must-Know Priorities

  • Airway first — facial burns, hoarseness, soot
  • Fluid replacement — Parkland formula, LR
  • Pain control — IV opioids only
  • Prevent infection — sterile wound care
  • Nutrition — high-protein, high-calorie
  • Psychosocial care — long-term support

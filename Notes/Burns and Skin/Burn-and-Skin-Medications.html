Burns and Skin

Burn & Skin Medications

Topical & systemic drugs for burns, wound care, and skin infections

1. Burn Management Medications

💧 IV Fluids — Lactated Ringer's

Used for: fluid resuscitation in major burns, especially the first 24 hours.

Side effects: fluid overload, electrolyte imbalance.

🧴 Silver Sulfadiazine

Used for: topical antibiotic for 2nd- and 3rd-degree burns.

Side effects: painless application; can decrease WBCs; not for face or pregnancy.

🦠 Mafenide (Sulfamylon)

Used for: electrical burns or eschar penetration.

Side effects: painful application, metabolic acidosis.

💉 Tetanus Vaccine

Used for: given if status unknown or outdated, to prevent tetanus infection in burns.

Side effects: fever, soreness.

4. Parasitic Skin Infections

🧴 Permethrin 1% or 5% Cream

Used for: lice (1%) or scabies (5%).

Side effects: burning, itching, redness after application.

NCLEX Special Notes

  • Always check for allergies to sulfa (e.g., silver sulfadiazine, mafenide)
  • Burn dressing changes are painful — pre-medicate if needed
  • Monitor for signs of infection in burns (↑ temp, odor, green drainage)
📝
NCLEX Summary

Silver sulfadiazine = painless, broad-spectrum, avoid on face/pregnancy. Mafenide = painful, penetrates eschar, watch for metabolic acidosis. Permethrin 5% = scabies; 1% = lice. Mupirocin = impetigo. Topical steroids = eczema/dermatitis, watch for skin thinning. Oral fluconazole = widespread fungal infection, watch liver function. Always screen for sulfa allergy before silver sulfadiazine or mafenide.

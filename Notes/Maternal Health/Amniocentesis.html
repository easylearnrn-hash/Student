Maternal Health

💉 Amniocentesis

Invasive prenatal diagnostic procedure — indications, nursing care, complications, and NCLEX priorities

← Back to Notes

What It Is & Why It Is Done

Amniocentesis is an invasive prenatal diagnostic procedure in which a small amount of amniotic fluid (fluid surrounding the fetus) is removed from the uterus using a needle.

It is usually performed at 15–20 weeks of gestation for genetic diagnosis.

Purposes Include

  • Diagnose chromosomal abnormalities such as Down syndrome (trisomy 21)
  • Diagnose inherited genetic disorders such as cystic fibrosis or sickle cell disease
  • Evaluate suspected fetal infection
  • Occasionally evaluate other fetal conditions
  • Therapeutically remove excess fluid in polyhydramnios (excessive amniotic fluid)
  • Fetal lung-maturity testing may appear in older NCLEX material but is now rarely performed

💡 Important

Amniocentesis is a diagnostic test, unlike prenatal screening tests, which only estimate risk.

How It Is Performed

  1. Pregnant patient lies on the examination table.
  2. Ultrasound (imaging using sound waves) identifies the fetus, placenta, and safe pocket of amniotic fluid.
  3. Abdomen is cleansed with antiseptic.
  4. Using continuous ultrasound guidance, the provider inserts a thin hollow needle through the abdominal wall into the uterus, avoiding the fetus and placenta when possible.
  5. A small amount of amniotic fluid is withdrawn.
  6. Needle is removed and fetal status is reassessed.

Pre-Procedure Nursing Care & Teaching

  • Verify informed consent has been obtained.
  • Explain that ultrasound guides the needle and the patient must remain still during needle insertion.
  • Obtain baseline maternal vital signs.
  • Assess baseline FHR (fetal heart rate).
  • Verify maternal blood type and Rh status (presence or absence of Rh antigen on red blood cells).
  • Review allergies, medications, bleeding disorders, anticoagulant use, and maternal infections.
  • Follow provider instructions regarding bladder filling/emptying; requirements may vary with gestational age and technique.
  • Explain that temporary pressure, stinging, or mild uterine cramping may occur.
  • Provide emotional support; genetic testing can cause significant anxiety.

Nurse's Role During the Procedure

  • Assist with positioning and sterile preparation.
  • Help maintain sterile technique.
  • Ensure the patient remains still.
  • Observe maternal condition and provide reassurance.
  • Assist with ultrasound/FHR monitoring as directed.
  • Watch for dizziness, pain, bleeding, contractions, or other signs of intolerance.
  • Correctly label and send the amniotic-fluid specimen to the laboratory.

Post-Procedure Nursing Care & Monitoring

Priority: Assess fetal and maternal well-being
  • Check FHR after the procedure.
  • Monitor maternal vital signs.
  • Assess for:
    • Vaginal bleeding
    • Amniotic-fluid leakage
    • Persistent/severe abdominal cramping
    • Uterine contractions
    • Fever
    • Changes in fetal movement when the patient is far enough along to perceive movement
  • Inspect the needle-insertion site if indicated.
  • Determine whether RhIg (Rh immune globulin — a medication preventing Rh sensitization) is required.

🚨 Rh-Negative Patient = Major NCLEX Point

An unsensitized Rh-negative patient generally receives Rh immune globulin after amniocentesis because fetal blood cells may enter the maternal circulation during the procedure.

Expected vs. Abnormal Findings

✅ Expected Normal

  • Mild temporary cramping
  • Mild soreness at needle site
  • Stable maternal vital signs
  • Reassuring fetal heart activity

⚠️ Abnormal Report Immediately

  • Vaginal bleeding
  • Leakage of clear fluid from the vagina
  • Severe or persistent uterine cramping/contractions
  • Fever or chills
  • Redness/drainage at insertion site
  • Significant decrease or absence of usual fetal movement
  • Concerning FHR changes

Major Complications & Nursing Actions

ComplicationNursing Action
Pregnancy loss / miscarriage — rare but importantAssess bleeding, pain, contractions, and fetal status; notify the provider immediately
Amniotic-fluid leakageAssess amount/color of vaginal fluid and notify the provider
InfectionWatch for fever, chills, uterine tenderness, or abnormal drainage → notify provider immediately
Rh sensitization (maternal formation of antibodies against Rh-positive fetal blood)For an eligible Rh-negative, unsensitized patient → administer prescribed RhIg
Fetal needle injuryRare because ultrasound guidance is used
Preterm contractions/laborAssess contractions and fetal status; notify provider promptly

Amniocentesis has a small procedure-related pregnancy-loss risk; risk is higher when performed too early in pregnancy.

Post-Procedure Patient Teaching

Tell the patient to contact the healthcare provider immediately for:

  • Vaginal bleeding
  • Clear-fluid leakage
  • Fever
  • Severe/persistent cramping or contractions
  • Decreased fetal movement

Follow provider instructions regarding activity. Avoid strenuous activity if specifically instructed; routine prolonged bed rest is not automatically required after an uncomplicated procedure.

Contraindications / Important Precautions

Amniocentesis requires careful risk assessment with:

  • Maternal blood-borne infections such as HIV or hepatitis because of possible fetal transmission
  • Significant bleeding disorders or anticoagulant therapy
  • Active vaginal/uterine infection
  • Significant vaginal bleeding or uterine contractions
  • Placental or fetal position interfering with safe needle placement
  • Very low amniotic-fluid volume

💡 Note

These are generally clinical precautions/relative contraindications, not universal absolute contraindications.

⭐ NCLEX Priority Points & Test Traps

🧠 High-Yield Points

  • After amniocentesis → check FHR. Fetal well-being is a priority.
  • Rh-negative + unsensitized + amniocentesis → think Rh immune globulin.
  • Ultrasound guides the needle — it helps avoid the fetus, placenta, and umbilical cord.
  • Amniocentesis = DIAGNOSTIC. Prenatal blood/cell-free DNA tests = primarily SCREENING.
  • Amniocentesis is generally performed ≥15 weeks. Earlier invasive genetic testing may involve CVS (chorionic villus sampling — sampling placental tissue).
  • Do NOT consider vaginal fluid leakage, significant bleeding, fever, severe cramping, or contractions normal after the procedure.

🧠 NCLEX Memory Line

AMNIO → Ultrasound + needle → monitor FHR → watch bleeding/fluid/contractions → Rh-negative patient may need RhIg.
